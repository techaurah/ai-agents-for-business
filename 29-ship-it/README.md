# Lesson 29: Ship it: present, publish, and showcase your plan

**Time:** about 90 minutes · **Difficulty:** Intermediate · **Prerequisites:** Lesson 28 · **You need:** Your stress tested plan from Lesson 28.

The course ends the way deployments begin: with the plan presented to the people who must live with it, in one sitting, against its gates. This lesson is the finishing routine. A final read of the complete plan by its owner, aloud where possible, because prose that cannot survive being read aloud will not survive an incident. A review meeting run like Lesson 22's gate: the plan's question, its thresholds, its costs, its residue, each presented by the person who will own it in operation, not by its author alone. Then publication: the plan, fictionalised where marked, goes into your fork and, if you choose, into this course's showcase, where it stands as the completion artifact this course offers instead of a certificate: evidence of judgement that a future client, employer, or partner can actually inspect.

## The release checklist

Ship nothing that fails its own checks. The routine, in order: run the repository validator on the plan file and the acceptance set one last time from a clean copy of the folder, because works on my machine is not a gate. Read the first page alone and confirm a stranger grasps the process, the question, the route, and the gate date in ninety seconds; rewrite the page until one does. Confirm every number in the plan traces to a tally, a quote, or a stated assumption, and that fictionalised figures are marked where changed. Confirm no real customer data appears anywhere, including in annexes and examples added late. Confirm the residue page and the review dates are present. Then hold the meeting, record the decision (proceed to pilot, revise and return, or do not deploy, all three respectable), and only then publish.

## What you have, and what it is worth

Step back from the plan and inventory the skill it represents, because the course's promise was judgement, transferable beyond this one process. You can now walk into any agent pitch, from any vendor, including the ones that do not exist yet, and ask the questions that sort the field: what kind is it really, where is the baseline, who operates it, what does supervision cost, show me the escalation on my hardest case, what does month thirteen look like, and how do I leave? You can also do the rarer thing: commission an agent well, in writing, with a pilot designed to tell the truth and a gate with the courage to hear it. The plan in the showcase is the receipt. The questions are the asset, and they compound every time your business is sold speed, certainty, or magic.

## The running example: BrightSmile Dental

BrightSmile's plan shipped as the worked example: validator clean from a fresh folder copy, first page rewritten twice (the first draft led with the vendor's name, a category error the ninety second test caught instantly, since the plan is about the practice's decision, not a product), numbers traced, annexes checked for the patient data that never belonged there and was not, residue page present with five carried risks. The review meeting took forty minutes, decided proceed to pilot on the original gate date, and assigned the plan's monthly review to the standing management meeting rather than a new appointment, because habits that need new meetings die. The plan then went to the showcase in fictionalised form, exactly as you are reading it across this course.

## Run it

Run `python3 release_check.py` on the worked plan in this lesson's capstone folder, then on your own. The checker covers the mechanical gates; the ninety second stranger test and the meeting are yours to run, and the lesson tells you how.

## Common failures

- **Publishing before the meeting..** The showcase is for decided plans. A plan published while its own business has not decided teaches the wrong lesson to every reader, including its author.
- **A first page about the product..** The plan's subject is a business decision: process, question, route, gate date. Vendor names belong in the route section, cited as evidence, not on the cover.
- **Skipping the clean copy check..** Run the validator from a fresh copy of the folder. Paths and habits from your working directory hide exactly the breakage a reader will meet.

## Recap checklist

- [ ] My plan passes its validators from a clean copy, and its first page passes the stranger test.
- [ ] The review meeting is held and its decision recorded, whichever way it went.
- [ ] My plan is published in my fork, fictionalised where marked, with its residue page intact.
