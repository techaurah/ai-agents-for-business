# Lesson 25: Expand, hand over, or stop: the second decision

**Time:** about 60 minutes · **Difficulty:** Intermediate · **Prerequisites:** Lessons 22 to 24 · **You need:** Your scorecard, unit economics, and monthly review habit.

A successful pilot creates a new risk: momentum. Scope expands because the agent seems cheap, hours expand because evenings went well, channels expand because a partner asked, and each expansion quietly re runs the original risks at a larger scale with none of the original ceremony. This lesson formalises the second decision, taken at a gate like the first, with three respectable outcomes. Expand: new scope, proposed as deltas against the existing artefacts (which map steps, which rungs, which data grades, what new cost lines), each delta gated like a small pilot. Hand over: the system is stable enough to move from project ownership to line ownership, operator steady state, vendor management in the monthly loop, and the project's owner formally lets go. Or stop: the honest end, for economics, for trust, or because the business changed, executed with the same care as a launch.

## Expansion is a new pilot wearing the old one's clothes

Score each proposed expansion against the original disciplines, briefly but really. Hours expansion (evenings to all day) mostly changes the comparison group and the operator rota; data first, ceremony light. Channel expansion (Instagram to phone transcripts, say) changes the evidence quality and the disclosure surface; acceptance cases must be added per channel. Scope expansion (standard path to insurance questions) is the dangerous one: it moves the bounded judgement frontier, so it needs Lesson 02's sampling on the new case types, Lesson 03's filter reapplied without nostalgia, and new acceptance cases before a single live case. The rule that keeps expansion honest: no expansion inherits the pilot's verdict. Each carries its own question, its own thresholds, its own gate date, proportionate to its risk.

## Stopping well, and handing over honestly

Stop is a deliverable, not an embarrassment, and it has a checklist: customers in flight are completed by humans, the fallback becomes the process again, data and logs are exported and the provider's copies deleted under the contract's schedule, the team is told plainly what was learned and what it cost, and the decision record preserves the evidence for the day a future vendor pitches the same idea to a future manager. Hand over has its own trap: the pilot's owner keeps informally supervising forever, which burns them out and starves the operator role of authority. The handover record names the steady state owner, the monthly loop as the control, and the conditions that would reopen project status (a scope expansion, an incident, a provider change). Both records are short. Both are the difference between a business that learns and a business that merely experiences.

## The running example: BrightSmile Dental

BrightSmile at month three faces all three doors at once, which is why the lesson exists. Expansion proposed: add the second location's enquiries (a volume and rota delta, light ceremony, approved as a delta with its own two week watch) and add insurance questions (a genuine scope frontier, sent back through sampling and the filter, deferred). Hand over: the standard path moves to line ownership under the operator, monthly loop as control, project owner formally out, recorded. Stop: the follow up message experiment within the pilot, which the scorecard showed added bookings too small to measure against its annoyance complaints, is stopped cleanly, customers in flight completed, and the record notes the learning, chase once means once was right. One meeting, three decisions, each with its own evidence standard: that is the second decision done properly.

## Run it

Run `python3 second_decision.py`. One of BrightSmile's five proposals needs the full gate, and it is the one that sounded smallest in the meeting: adding a question type moves the judgement frontier, while adding a whole location does not.

## Common failures

- **Expansion by momentum..** Seems cheap, went well, partner asked: none of these is evidence. Deltas get gates proportionate to the frontier they move.
- **A stop with no record..** Unrecorded stops get re pitched within a year by a new vendor to a new manager, at full price. The decision record is the business's immune system.
- **Handover that never happens..** If the project owner still supervises informally, the operator has responsibility without authority and the owner has a second job without a rota. Record the handover, then honour it.

## Recap checklist

- [ ] Every expansion I consider is classified by the frontier it moves, with a matching gate.
- [ ] Stops in my business produce decision records with evidence preserved.
- [ ] Handover names the steady state owner and the conditions that reopen project status.
